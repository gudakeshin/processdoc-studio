# Approach note — leading practices (hypothesis-led)

Approach notes answer **how we should tackle the problem** and **why our path is plausible**, without being a full proposal or a status report.

## Consulting-grade posture

1. **Argument before activity** — The reader should understand the causal story (why the usual fix fails, what you believe instead) before seeing workstreams.
2. **Explicit evidence grade** — Tag claims: *Fact (from client)*, *Inference*, *Hypothesis*, *Unknown / needs validation*.
3. **Decision-centric close** — End with what decision you need from whom (steer/co, funding, methodology fork), on what timeline.
4. **Intellectual honesty** — Name disconfirming evidence and what would change your mind.

## Section reinforcement (maps to the 7-section template)

| Section | Leading-practice focus |
|---------|-------------------------|
| Problem framing | Stakeholders, constraints, success definition; sharpen to a decision problem |
| Why standard approach fails | Non-generic critique (incentive, data, org, tech, vendor pattern), not “lack of leadership” clichés |
| Hypothesis | Falsifiable; causal mechanism; what must be true |
| Proposed approach | Work streams as **tests** of the hypothesis, not a generic phase plan |
| What we learn when | Milestones that reduce uncertainty; metrics or artifacts |
| Risks & limitations | Epistemic risks + delivery risks; what you will not know until later |
| Conclusion | Clear ask; alternatives considered briefly |

## Distinctions

- **vs proposal** — Proposals price and sell; approach notes debate and align on *how*.
- **vs BRD** — BRDs specify requirements; approach notes do not lock requirements until method is accepted.

## SAP / transformation tie-in

When the context is SAP or finance transformation, name the **standard-vs-fit** fork, data readiness, and governance model as core uncertainties to resolve in Section 05.
