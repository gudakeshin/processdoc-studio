# Story Arc — Five-Step Narrative Structure

Finance transformation proposals build credibility through problem-led storytelling, not credential inflation.

---

## 1. Client situation — Open with finance pain, not firm history

**NOT THIS:** "Deloitte has transformed 500+ finance functions globally."

**THIS:** "Your month-end close has grown from 8 days to 14 days over the past three years, blocking timely P&L for the CFO's strategic planning cycle."

**Why:** The client cares about their problem, not your CV.

### Structure

- Lead with the observable operating challenge (close calendar, forecast volatility, FTE cost, control failures)
- Quantify impact if possible (days added, headcount, cost per transaction)
- Show you understand the context (growth, system landscape, prior initiatives)
- Avoid "we've seen this before" at this stage

### Examples by engagement type

**Close Acceleration**
- Close calendar: 12 days → industry benchmark 4 days (8-day gap)
- Manual GL reconciliation: 45% of month-end effort
- Audit issues: 6 SAP-related control findings in last review

**FP&A Transformation**
- Budget cycle: 6 weeks → planning need 3 weeks (miss strategic decisions)
- Forecast accuracy: ±8% vs. best practice ±2%
- Scenario modeling: 3-5 days per scenario (limiting "what-if" analysis)

**GBS / Operating Model Redesign**
- Finance cost: 1.2% of revenue vs. benchmark 0.6%
- Headcount per $B revenue: 45 FTE vs. benchmark 20 FTE
- Shared services penetration: 30% vs. target 70%

**Treasury Transformation**
- Cash visibility: Overnight or next-day vs. intraday need
- Working capital cycle: DSO 55 days, DPO 35 days vs. target DSO 40, DPO 50
- Payment processing: 40% manual intervention in exception handling

## 2. Insight — What's misunderstood about root cause

Root causes fall into five categories: **organization, data, technology, process, incentives.**

Don't assume technology is the problem. In 70% of finance transformations, process and organization are bigger levers than systems.

### Organization
- Siloed close process (each region/BU owns their month-end)
- Insufficient ownership (close owner is 10% role, not dedicated)
- Career path problem (no advancement in transactional finance, talent leaves)
- Governance vacuum (no steering committee, approval authority unclear)

### Data
- Master data quality: GL account hierarchy fragmented, GL duplicates exist
- Subledger-to-GL reconciliation manual (supplier master has 40% duplicates)
- Manual data entry in close (journal entries, consolidation adjustments)
- Chart of accounts inconsistency (different cost center hierarchies by region)

### Technology
- GL system (SAP, Oracle) underutilized (manual workarounds exist)
- Manual GL reconciliation tools (Excel-based, 40% of close effort)
- Siloed systems (cash ledger not integrated with main GL)
- Legacy systems blocking automation (custom interfaces, batch jobs)

### Process
- Too many approval steps (6-8 sign-offs for month-end close)
- Manual task sequence (cannot parallelize; sequential bottlenecks)
- No automation of routine transactions (recurring entries still manual)
- Unclear close procedures (process documented informally or in email)

### Incentives
- Close speed not a KPI (CFO doesn't reward early close; delays not visible)
- Accuracy over speed (culture of manual verification; automation seen as risky)
- People incentivized to maintain current process (security, visibility)
- No penalty for overrun (late closes don't escalate)

### Template

"The close calendar has extended from **[prior state]** to **[current state]** primarily because **[root cause]**, not **[common misconception]**. Specifically:

- **[Organization factor]**: [Evidence]
- **[Data factor]**: [Evidence]
- **[Process factor]**: [Evidence]"

Don't list all five categories; pick the 2-3 that matter. Don't blame "legacy systems" without evidence.

## 3. Hypothesis — What will move the needle

Crisp, testable hypothesis. One or two sentences.

**NOT THIS:** "We will digitize and modernize your finance function through best-in-class automation and cloud technologies."

**THIS:** "We believe reducing close calendar from 12 days to 6 days is achievable in 4 months by automating GL reconciliation (eliminating 40% of manual effort) and restructuring the approval workflow to enable parallel execution (enabling 2-day compression)."

### Template

"We believe **[target outcome]** is achievable in **[timeframe]** by **[2-3 specific interventions]**, with an estimated **[value]** impact."

### Value estimation (order of magnitude)
- **Close acceleration**: 1-2 days per month → CFO decision speed improvement, visibility, external reporting faster, better working capital decisions
- **FP&A transformation**: 2-3 weeks cycle time saved → enables more scenarios, better strategic decisions, improved planning accuracy
- **GBS transition**: 0.3-0.5% of revenue saved + centralization benefits + quality improvement
- **Working capital**: 5-10% DSO/DPO improvement → cash release ($10M-$50M for typical mid-cap)
- **Forecast accuracy**: ±5% to ±2% improvement → better capital allocation, reduced surprises

### Red flags (hypotheses that won't work)

- "We will replace your GL with a new cloud system" (tech-first, not problem-first)
- "We will automate everything" (impossible; manual judgment always needed)
- "We will eliminate 30% of finance headcount" (oversells savings; ignores retention risk)
- "Your close will be 1 day" (unrealistic; best-in-class is 2-4 days, not 1)

## 5. Why us — Evidence, proportional

**NOT THIS (credential inflation):**
- Deloitte has 15,000 consultants
- We've done 500+ transformations
- We won industry awards
- Our global network is world-class

**THIS (evidence-backed):**
"For a similar $2B specialty chemicals company, we reduced close from 12 to 6 days while improving GL reconciliation accuracy from 92% to 99%, delivered in 4 months. Our team has led 8+ close acceleration transformations at companies of your scale in the past 3 years."

### Rule of thumb
"Why us" should be 5-10% of the document, not 30%. One strong case pattern + brief team credential statement.

### Structure

1. **One concrete case pattern** with quantified outcome (see [case-patterns.md](../templates/case-patterns.md))
2. **Team credential statement** (e.g., "Led 8+ close transformations at mid-market manufacturers")
3. **Unique point of view** (optional; only if genuinely differentiated)

Avoid: "We have deep expertise in finance," "We understand your challenges," "We've worked with leading companies."

---

## Tying it together

The five-step arc answers these questions in order:

1. **Client situation** → "Do they really understand my problem?"
2. **Insight** → "Do they understand why it's happening, not just that it's happening?"
3. **Hypothesis** → "Do they have a clear, testable point of view on how to fix it?"
4. **What we will do** → "Can they actually deliver this? What do I need to commit?"
5. **Why us** → "Have they done this before successfully? Why should I trust them?"

If any link breaks, credibility drops.
